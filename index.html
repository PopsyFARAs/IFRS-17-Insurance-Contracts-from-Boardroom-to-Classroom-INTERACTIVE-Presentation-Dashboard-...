Interactive · Scope wizard

Is this contract inside IFRS 17?

In scope · ¶ 3
  • Insurance contracts, including reinsurance contracts, an entity issues
  • Reinsurance contracts it holds
  • Investment contracts with discretionary participation features it issues, provided it also issues insurance contracts
Out of scope · ¶ 7
  • Product warranties issued by a manufacturer, dealer or retailer
  • Employers' assets and liabilities from employee benefit plans
  • Contractual rights or obligations contingent on future use of a non-financial item (for example some licence fees, royalties, variable lease payments)
  • Residual value guarantees by a manufacturer, dealer or retailer, and a lessee's residual value guarantee in a lease
  • Some financial guarantee contracts (choice under ¶ 7(e))
  • Contingent consideration in a business combination
  • Insurance contracts in which the entity is the policyholder, unless they are reinsurance contracts held
  • Some credit card and similar contracts (¶ 7(h))
Choice · ¶ 8 and 8A

Fixed-fee service contracts and loan-type contracts

An entity may choose IFRS 15 for certain fixed-fee service contracts, and IFRS 9 or IFRS 17 for contracts whose only insurance is settlement of the policyholder's obligation.

Level of aggregation, recognition and contract boundary

The CSM is tracked for groups, not single policies. Portfolio, then annual cohort, then profitability bucket. Once a contract is placed in a group, it stays there.

Contract boundary · ¶ 34

Which cash flows belong in the measurement?

  • Inside: cash flows from substantive rights and obligations in the reporting period, where the entity can compel the policyholder to pay premiums, or has a substantive obligation to provide services.
  • The obligation ends when the entity can reassess risks and set a price that fully reflects them, for the individual policyholder or for the portfolio (¶ 34(a) and (b)).
  • Outside: expected premiums or claims beyond that point. They belong to future contracts, not this one (¶ 35).
Module 05 · Understand

Measurement models and the building blocks

The general measurement model (GMM, also called the building block approach) is the default. The variable fee approach (VFA) is mandatory for direct participating contracts. The premium allocation approach (PAA) is an optional simplification.

¶ 29 to 52 GMM¶ 45, B101 to B118 VFA¶ 53 to 59 PAA
See the building blocks riseTouch the model chooserName the one-page model
Concrete firstBuilding a house: the foundation is your best estimate of cash flows, the walls adjust for the time value of money, the roof is the risk adjustment that keeps you dry when estimates are wrong, and the CSM is the unearned rent you collect only as tenants live in the house.
See it · Building blocks (GMM)

The liability, block by block

1 · Estimates of future cash flows¶ 33 to 35
2 · Adjustment for time value and financial risk¶ 36
3 · Risk adjustment for non-financial risk¶ 37
4 · Contractual service margin¶ 38

Blocks 1 to 3 are the fulfilment cash flows. Add block 4 and you have the insurance contract liability for a profitable group.

Discount rates: top-down, bottom-up and the OCI option

Discount rates reflect the time value of money, the characteristics of the cash flows and the liquidity of the contracts, and stay consistent with observable market prices. Both approaches are permitted and should converge.

Module 10 · Model Lab · FinPolNomics note on risk adjustments

Risk adjustment: deterministic and stochastic approaches

The risk adjustment is the compensation the entity requires for bearing non-financial uncertainty. IFRS 17 does not prescribe a technique, but if you do not use a confidence level you must disclose the confidence level your result corresponds to.

¶ 37B86 to B92¶ 119 confidence level disclosure
Touch four techniquesSee the simulated distributionName the confidence level
Concrete firstDriving from Ikeja to Victoria Island usually takes 50 minutes. You leave 30 minutes earlier "just in case". Those 30 minutes are your risk adjustment: the price you put on uncertainty about traffic, not on the average trip.

Reinsurance contracts held

Consistent assumptions with the underlying contracts, a risk adjustment for the risk transferred to the reinsurer, and a CSM that can be a net cost or a net gain. The 2020 amendments add a loss-recovery component when the underlying group is onerous.

Module 13 · Model Lab · simplified illustration

Variable fee approach against the GMM

A unit-linked style fund pays the policyholder the fund value; the insurer keeps an annual charge. Under the VFA the insurer's share of market movements flows through the CSM. Under the GMM the same movement would hit the finance result. Shock the market and compare.

¶ 45B101 to B118IE100 to IE112
Touch yearly market returnsSee two CSM pathsName the variable fee
Concrete firstA fund manager earns 2% of whatever the fund is worth. When the market rises, next year's fees rise too. Under the VFA, that change in expected fees is unearned profit for future service, so it goes into the CSM rather than straight to profit.
Closing CSM each year · VFA against GMM

Teaching simplification: expected fees are valued at the expected return; risk adjustment and expenses are held constant; coverage units are equal by year. Real VFA measurement also covers guarantees, risk mitigation (¶ B115) and the full fulfilment cash flows.
